Welcome to Aotearoa

New Zealand is an island country in the South Pacific Ocean. It has two main islands, the North Island and the South Island. In te reo Māori, it is called Aotearoa, which means "land of the long white cloud".

This website introduces some of the most popular cities in New Zealand. Some are busy and modern, while others are small and close to mountains, lakes and beaches.

North Island Cities

The North Island is home to most of New Zealand's people. It has warm beaches, volcanoes and geothermal areas.

Auckland

Auckland is the largest city in New Zealand. It is built between two harbours and is known as the City of Sails. Visitors can go up the Sky Tower, walk up Mt Eden, or take a ferry to Waiheke Island.

Wellington

Wellington is the capital city, where Parliament meets in a building called the Beehive. It is famous for Te Papa, the national museum, and for its strong winds. The city centre is small and easy to explore on foot.

Rotorua

Rotorua is known for its geysers, hot pools and bubbling mud. It is also an important centre of Māori culture, where visitors can watch traditional performances and learn about Māori history.

South Island Cities

The South Island is larger than the North Island but has fewer people. It is famous for the Southern Alps, glaciers, lakes and wildlife.

Christchurch

Christchurch is the largest city in the South Island. It is called the Garden City because of its many parks. Visitors can go punting on the Avon River or visit the International Antarctic Centre.

Queenstown

Queenstown sits beside Lake Wakatipu, with the Remarkables mountains behind it. It is known as the adventure capital of New Zealand, with bungy jumping, jet boating, skiing and hiking.

Dunedin

Dunedin was settled by Scottish people, and its name comes from the old name for Edinburgh. It is a university city with old stone buildings. Nearby, visitors can see albatross, seals and penguins.
